About the rule

At the close of each month's last trading day, two tests:

  • Trend: is SPY's month-end close above its average over the last 10 month-ends?
  • Credit: is the Baa corporate spread over Treasuries (BAA10Y, as published for the prior day) at or below its 126-day average?

Both pass → SSO (2x S&P 500). One passes → SPY. Neither → T-bills (BIL). The trade happens at the next session's close.

The record (backtests, not this account): 1989–2025, about 14.4% a year against 11.3% for the S&P 500, with a worst drawdown of 38.8% against 55.2%. It passed the 2005–2025 test and the 1989–2004 out-of-sample test, and the 2026-01-02 to 2026-05-19 holdout smoke test.

Caveats: it has bad stretches (2018 and 2022 were both well behind the index), and a short holdout already trailed SPY. SSO's leverage costs money: financing, a 0.91% expense ratio, and daily-reset decay in choppy markets. Monthly switching realizes gains, so taxes matter outside a tax-free account. This paper account probably credits no distributions, which understates the T-bill and SPY legs.
