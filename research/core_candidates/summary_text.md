Over 2005–2025, with total returns, costs and financing included, **three of the five rules beat SPY on all three tests**: C4 (credit-filtered
2x), C5 (trend + credit) and C1 (trend-filtered 2x). C2 (volatility targeting) tied SPY on return and lost; C3 (dual momentum) clearly lost.

- **C4 has the highest CAGR (13.85% vs SPY's 10.64%) and is the only one that goes to the holdout.** $100,000 became about $1.52M against
  SPY's $0.84M, with a smaller worst drawdown (41.9% vs 55.2%). It won all three sub-periods. It works by sitting in T-bills when corporate
  credit spreads are widening (44% of the time) and holding 2x S&P when they are calm. That is how it was mostly out of 2008 and all of
  2022 (−9% that year vs SPY's −18%).
- **C5 is almost as good and steadier** (13.71% CAGR, the smallest drawdown of the passes at 38.8%, all three sub-periods). **C1** passes
  more narrowly: it lost the 2019–2025 sub-period and had the worst year of any passing rule (−39.5% in 2022).

**Treat these passes with real caution. They are hypotheses, not a finding.**

- **C4's result depends on one setting.** With a 63-day credit average instead of 126, it falls to 9.8% CAGR with a 56% drawdown, worse
  than SPY. With 252 days it beats SPY's CAGR but wins only one sub-period. A rule that only works at one of three reasonable settings may
  have been lucky on this one path. C5 is less exposed: it passes at every cost level tested, but its own credit window was not varied.
- **Five rules were tried on the same 21 years**, and 2008 was the kind of slow bear market these filters are best at. One or two clearing
  the bar by chance is quite possible.
- **These strategies are much rougher to live with than SPY.** They had losing years when SPY rose: C4 −8.6% in 2007, −5.3% in 2015 and
  −18.6% in 2018; C1 −18.4% in 2015. And 2x exposure doubles every bad month while it is on.
- **No taxes.** Monthly switching only makes sense in a tax-free account.

The 2x simulation is trustworthy as a model: it tracked the real SSO fund to within 0.04 points a year on average (daily correlation 0.996).
The independent verifier matched all 60 re-derived checks.
