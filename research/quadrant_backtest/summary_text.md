**The pre-registered verdicts are both "no": CALLS ADD NO VALUE and DOES NOT BEAT SPY. But the picture underneath is mixed, and the
verdicts turn on one thing: the last five years.**

- **Over the whole window (2018-01-19 to 2025-12-31) the quadrant tilt did well on paper.** Q-TILT grew 14.9% a year, against 11.5% for
  holding all four baskets (Q-ALL) and 13.6% for SPY. Its worst drawdown was 14.4%, against SPY's 33.7%. If the test were CAGR and
  drawdown alone, it would pass.
- **It fails because the gain is concentrated in 2018–2020.** Q-TILT made +84.7% there, against SPY's +41.0%. In 2021–2025 it made
  +63.0%, behind both Q-ALL (+83.2%) and SPY (+95.4%). The rule needed a win in both periods: the first sub-period (to 2015) is empty,
  because the classifier can't be reconstructed before 2016. It won one of two.
- **Much of 2018–2020 is one well-timed call.** On data available on 2020-01-17, the classifier moved to Q4 (gold, Treasuries, staples,
  healthcare, min-vol). It held that through the COVID crash, then moved to Q1 in July 2020, in time for the rally. That call was made in
  real time with no lookahead, but it is one call before an external shock, not repeated evidence of skill. Since 2021 the calls have lagged.
- **The hit table is mixed.** The Q1, Q2 and Q3 baskets did better while they were the call than while they weren't. The Q4 (defensive)
  basket did worse while it was the call (+1.9% a year vs +8.8% otherwise), so the defensive calls cost money on average outside 2020.
- **The classifier is usually undecided.** It gave a determinate quadrant on only 23% of days (37% since 2016). The rest of the time
  Q-TILT held the last call it had, which is how it switched 67 times in eight years.

**What this means for Core:** this is a test of the pure calls (100% in one basket), not of the live book, which blends the reference
with floors, caps, a cash sleeve and overrides. On this evidence the framework's calls are not a reliable edge: they helped a lot in one
crisis and have trailed a plain mix of the same baskets, and SPY, for five years. Next to the earlier result, C5 (trend + credit) was the
more robust rule over 1989–2025, but on this short window C5 itself trailed SPY (12.0% vs 13.6%), which shows how much one 8-year path
depends on its start date.
